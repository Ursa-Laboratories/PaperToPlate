2. Resolve the Bradford-specific assumptions
The reference method is Ernst and Zor, Linearization of the Bradford Protein Assay: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3164080/ . It uses measurements at 590 and 450 nm, a water-only optical blank, and a zero-protein dye well retained in the calibration. Does our reader support the method? Who will operate it and export the raw readings?
Which exact Bradford reagent do we have: manufacturer, product, stock concentration and lot? The current draft assumes the paper's reagent diluted 2.5-fold. Confirm that this is appropriate for the actual product; do not dilute a ready-to-use formulation by assumption.

Available paper choices for tomorrow
The application now includes a Morning paper library with the 16-paper review shortlist and locally available source PDFs, including supporting information where recovered. The extracted method drafts remain unreviewed scientific proposals.
Keep the two Bradford methods separate. The original JoVE method uses a 200 µL assay and A590/A450 ratios. The recovered RotiQuant protocol uses 50 µL of prepared standard plus 200 µL working reagent, five minutes at room temperature and OD595. Its source gives an explicit working-reagent recipe of 4 mL reagent plus 11 mL water. Do not combine the reagent preparation, blank convention, volumes or readout from these different methods.
First discussion: which formulation and reader match the reagents and equipment actually in the lab? The RotiQuant source also leaves mixing, tip policy, usable stock reserve and acceptance limits for us to review. The cydex blue paper is a second protein-assay option when detergent compatibility matters, but its exact cyclodextrin condition must be selected from the paper.
Advanced cases should be deferred until their equipment and sample needs are clear. The dust-particle DTT module needs timed incubations and has a stated well volume above our current 300 µL plate setting. Cell-culture and clinical-sample methods are not quick substitutes for a first plate-preparation reference.
