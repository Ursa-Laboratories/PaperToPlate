Peroxide mixture quantification

Extracted scope

The three iodide-based microplate assays for PMS, hydrogen peroxide and PDS. Begins with prepared samples and standardized peroxide stocks; electrolysis and iodometric stock standardization are outside this extracted assay module.

Why selected: Explicit sample/reagent transfers, matched matrix blanks, replicate requirements, temperature and time-resolved readings are all in the same Methods page.

Details needing review 5

  • Conflicting phosphate-stock concentration

    The stock paragraph says 0.1 M, while both mix recipes specify 0.5 M. The 68 g/L recipe is also consistent with approximately 0.5 M KH₂PO₄. The extraction preserves this conflict instead of silently choosing a value.

    PDF p. 5

  • Stock-versus-final concentration labels

    The recipe adds indicator stock to sample, diluting it. Results label conditions with the same concentrations as those indicator stocks. Clarify whether these are stock or final-well labels; do not change either recipe silently.

    PDF p. 5 · PDF p. 6 · PDF p. 8

  • Fast timing and unspecified mixing

    PMS is measured within 0–10 seconds; the reported initial read was after about 3–5 seconds. Mixing method, speed and duration are absent. Dispensing/read latency must preserve the stated kinetic window.

    PDF p. 5 · PDF p. 6 · PDF p. 8

  • Calibration and stock verification need supporting detail

    The paper refers to SI for titration and Table S1. Those separate supplements are not included with this selected main-article PDF. Exact calibration preparation and dilution accounting need review. The authors explicitly say calibration depends on the container/path length.

    PDF p. 5 · PDF p. 6

  • Molybdate concentration basis

    The salt contains seven Mo atoms. Preserve the stated mass, solvent volume and “35 mM” label; confirm the intended molarity basis rather than assuming salt molarity.

    PDF p. 5

Extracted scope

Details needing review 5

  • Printed loading-volume unit is incomplete

    The visual PDF says “150 L”. The proposed 150 µL interpretation is plausible for a microplate but is explicitly not treated as a resolved source value.

    PDF p. 4

  • Lucigenin concentration conflicts

    The procedure says 100 µM internal lucigenin; Figure 1 labels 1 mM. This tenfold difference must be reconciled from the supplementary method or authors.

    PDF p. 4

  • Upstream preparation and calibration are external

    Vesicle manufacture, the transporter dilution series, reference transporter identity/dose, Stern–Volmer coefficients and Equation S2 are not fully specified in the selected main-PDF procedure. The separate SI is not in the 100-file ZIP.

    PDF p. 4

  • Plate capacity and unspecified parameters

    The conditional total is 303 µL, not 300 µL. Plate working volume, mixing parameters, temperature, exact well mapping and wells per concentration require decisions; none are invented here.

    PDF p. 4 · PDF p. 6

  • Vesicle age affects the signal

    The paper warns that leakage can reach 20% within two hours and later discusses use within 2–3 hours. A hard validity window should be reviewed for the chosen vesicle batch.

Extracted scope

The standard nucleoside-kinase reaction followed by the Kinase-Glo Max residual-ATP readout, using the human deoxycytidine kinase branch as the concrete example. Enzyme production, HPLC validation and optimized conditions for other enzymes remain separate.

Why selected: A defined 150 µL enzyme reaction feeds an explicit 10 + 80 + 10 µL luminescence assay, with controls, incubation conditions and replicate counts.

Materials and starting conditions

InputAs reported / unresolvedPDF p.
NK reaction buffer70 mM Tris, pH 7.6; 10 mM MgCl₂; 5 mM DTT.11
ATP and nucleosideFinal reaction concentrations 0.4 mM ATP and 0.33 mM nucleoside. General stocks are described as 100, 10 or 1 mM in deionized water, without assigning the exact stock to every reaction.10–11
EnzymeStandard reaction contains 0.0002 U enzyme per 150 µL. Human deoxycytidine kinase (HsdCK/NK13) is an available branch; stock activity in U/µL is not given.10–11

K09

Apply the reported control calculation

As printed: consumed ATP (%) = 100 − 100 × (R + B + S)/(N + S). R is mean reaction luminescence, N is mean no-enzyme-control luminescence, S is substrate-control signal, and B is the difference between negative-control and basal-activity signals. Product formation (%) = consumed ATP (%) × 1.2, restricted to 0–100%. These are the paper’s equations, not calculations on experimental data.

How this extraction was checked

The three selected papers are different from the earlier Bradford, DPPH and beer selection. Each numbered step includes a PDF page and a short source excerpt; the key Methods pages were also visually inspected.

The selected source PDFs are copied byte-for-byte from the downloaded ZIP. Their hashes and the structured extraction are included. Separate supporting-information files were not present for these three papers. This review does not substitute outside recipes or silently infer missing stock volumes, temperature settings or units.
